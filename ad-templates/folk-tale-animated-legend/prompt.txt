A brand origin legend told as an Indian folk tale in flat folk-art cut-out puppet animation (Cheriyal and Kalamkari painted worlds, Tholu Bommalata-style jointed puppets). Rich earthy colours on painted backdrops framed by a decorated border. Every element has ONE uniform black outline weight. Puppets move with jointed, slightly stiff limbs, like real cut-out puppets, never smooth 3D.

Structure:
1. Once upon a time: a happy kingdom, villagers at play (kites, market), a wide painted establishing frame.
2. The trouble: a colourful creature or problem arrives and demands something; the villagers are worried.
3. The failed attempts: ordinary offerings are refused.
4. The maker: a humble local cook or craftsperson makes the product, tossing its real ingredients (shown clearly, in the brand colour).
5. The twist: the creature tastes it and is won over; a small playful moment (a wink, a dance).
6. The name or promise is born; end on the product in the painted world with the tagline.

Camera: mostly flat, frontal storybook framing with slow pans across the painted world, like a scroll unrolling; each scene is one clear tableau.
Edit: scene changes as paper slides, folds or a scroll turning, never morphs.
Sound: a narrator (a warm elder storyteller voice) tells the tale; classical Indian music (Carnatic or Hindustani) changing mood with each beat, ending on a short rhythmic flourish as the logo lands.
Avoid: realistic 3D rendering, mixed outline weights, scary or violent moments, any text painted into the frames other than the end.
