# Genre: Food and beverage

**Evidence:**
- 420 famous-brand ads in the Pitt dataset (McDonald's, Burger King, Coca-Cola, Pepsi, Snickers, Knorr, Cadbury, Mentos, Haribo, Wendy's, Folgers and others);
- case studies: Cadbury "Gorilla" and Snickers "You're not you when you're hungry";
- food-cinematography guides.

Full analysis: `../sources/genre-datasets/food_beverage-dataset-analysis.md`.

## What wins

- **Pick one lane**, and choose from these:
  - **a product truth** (Wendy's "Where's the beef?");
  - **a functional benefit** (Snickers fixes your hunger and mood; Orbit cleans your mouth);
  - **an emotional transformation** (Coca-Cola "brings enemies together"; Haribo "makes the manliest men feel like children again").
- **Humour is strongest here.** The effective (5/5) ads averaged a funny score of **0.79**, against 0.67 for weak ones, and the dominant sentiment is *amused*. Excitement matters a little (0.60 vs 0.55).
- **Commit to one feeling.** Emotional (Folgers "Peter Comes Home") and calm, inclusive (McDonald's "Come As You Are") ads also score 5/5. Weak ads have a muddled mood.
- **The devices:**
  - **a celebrity as archetype** (Drake *is* Sprite's spark; Peyton Manning is Gatorade's performance);
  - **mascots** (M&M's, the California Raisins);
  - **a simple demo** (Knorr's "how to make…" recipes: "it will enhance the flavour");
  - **a tiny story** (Life cereal's "Mikey likes it" shows picky eaters like it).
- **Brand strategy by position:**
  - **Challenger** (Burger King): superiority, value, customisation ("Have it your way"); irreverent, and it attacks.
  - **Incumbent** (McDonald's): experience and emotion, inclusion and moments ("I'm lovin' it").
  - **Snickers:** one idea, endless executions (Betty White, Mr T, Joe Pesci, Mr Bean).
- **Cadbury "Gorilla":** pure emotion and an unexpected premise, with the brand held until the end. It took 60% recognition in 3 weeks, won the Cannes Grand Prix, and lifted sales 7–10% after a crisis. This works for a famous brand with strong assets (the purple and the glass and a half).

## Rules

1. **One proposition.** Not taste and health and togetherness; pick one.
2. **Make the benefit a sensation.** York Peppermint Pattie: "a satisfying cooling sensation".
3. **Prove superiority visually** (Wendy's shows the tiny patty).
4. **Make the tagline the strategy in disguise:** "Have it your way", "You're not you when you're hungry".
5. **If you use humour, commit fully.** A half-joke is worse than none (Carlton Draught "Big Ad" and "Beer Chase").
6. **Show the food in motion** (Ogilvy): being poured, smeared, cooked, broken or sizzling. **Let viewers hear it:** the crunch, the fizz, the pour.
7. **Show the pack or product clearly,** with brand colours throughout.

## Craft

- **Appetite appeal:**
  - a **hero shot** with the food filling the frame on a clean background;
  - **macro texture** (crumbs, oil, bubbles, condensation);
  - **steam and motion** (to suggest heat and freshness);
  - **slow-motion pours, pulls and drops.**
- **Lighting:** warm (2700–3500K) for comfort food, cooler (5000K+) for fresh and crisp food. Saturated but true colour.
- **Sound design is half the appetite:** crunch, pour, sizzle, a can opening, ice.
- **AI notes:**
  - Food drifts toward photoreal or plastic. Restate the medium, and the real pack must be **composited, not drawn** (as with KoriKey).
  - Liquids and pours are Veo's hardest motion, so keep them short (about 1–2s) and take several attempts.
  - Moving the light across a static hero plate is easy and reliable.
  - Faceless works well: hands at most, or just the product.
