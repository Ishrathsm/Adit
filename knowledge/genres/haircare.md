# Haircare (shampoo) ads

Research for the Aurelle film, 2026-10-07. Evidence is thin: most ad pages block fetches or hide transcripts. **Verified** means stated on a page that was read. **Inferred** means a category pattern that still needs checking against 5–6 real spots.

## Structure of a 30s spot
- **Inferred skeleton:**
  - 0–4s: a problem cue, such as a dull or frayed strand, a comb with hair in it, or a question super;
  - 4–12s: a named technology with a science sequence;
  - 12–20s: proof or a demo, such as lather, a hand through hair, or a claim super;
  - 20–26s: the result, with hair in motion and shine;
  - 26–30s: the packshot, the claim, and a disclaimer.
- **Verified:** Pantene's Meta ads lean on demos (dispensing and lather close-ups), before/after split screens and headline supers, under bright diffused light (https://motionapp.com/library/pantene-north-america).
- **Verified:** Pantene "Vitamin" travels inside a single strand, then returns to flowing hair, a micro-to-macro structure. Its CGI hair was built to match the live action (https://onesal.com/work/pantene-vitamin).

## How brands explain features
- **The strand "science sequence"** (Pantene): an uneven cuticle at macro scale, droplets merging into protein strands, gold for Pro-V. It runs 3–6s and needs no face. It's the most relevant device, but Pantene used bespoke CGI. **Don't ask Veo for it.** Build it as a flat 2D diagram in the edit.
- **A named technology:** Pantene "Pro-V", Dove "Bond Strength" with "Bio-Protein Care" and peptides, Olaplex "Bond Building Technology", Elvive "Bond Repair".
  - **Dove already uses "Bio-Protein Care"**, so a new brand needs its own name for its complex.
- **Numeric supers:** "up to 97% less breakage" (Pantene 2012, https://www.ispot.tv/ad/7Ve_/pantene-anti-breakage-shampoo-featuring-eva-mendes), and Dove's "97% less breakage" with a test-condition footnote. These **need lab substantiation; never invent numbers.**
- **Scientific voice-over:** Dove "strengthen your hair at a cellular level", Elvive "nothing repairs damaged hair better". These are clinical-sounding and need substantiation.
- **Authority:** Pantene India cites the Swiss Vitamin Institute and a stylist (https://www.afaqs.com/company-briefs/2216_pantene-launches-hair-fall-control-shampoo). Skip this without a real credential.

## Purpose and problem
- Pantene India framed the problem with a research number ("56% of Indian women suffer hair fall due to breakage") and added a celebrity payoff.
- Pantene Pro-V Miracles (US) opens on a question: tired of breakage?
- **Inferred** dramatisations: hair in a brush, a frayed strand close-up, a rough cuticle. The emotional payoff is confidence and the swing of the hair.

## Clichés and white space
- **Clichés:** a slow-motion hair flip, a glossy golden pour, a golden "Pro-V" glow, "up to 97%", "repairs damage", "salon quality at home", a celebrity.
- **White space for a science-led newcomer:**
  - each ingredient gets one named job, rather than one vague "technology";
  - an all-white, photoreal macro-led look, in a category that's mostly gold;
  - showing what's *not* inside (sulphate-free) as a visual line;
  - a purpose line that names the problem plainly.

## Faceless feasibility
- **Motion graphics in the edit:** supers and the end card only. A flat 2D strand diagram was tried for Aurelle and rejected because it clashed with the photoreal film; explain ingredients with real macro shots plus supers.
- **Veo handles these well:** hair from behind, a hand on the pump, lather macros, oil droplets, the packshot, hair in motion, and a comb through damp hair.
- **These look fake from Veo:** CGI strand morphs and "bonds relinking", counters or text inside the video, hair under tension, before/after splits (Veo won't hold identical hair across shots), and pours that read as wasted product.
