# Genre: Apps, services, finance and education

**Evidence:**
- 55 famous-brand ads in the Pitt dataset (Google, Mastercard, Visa, Squarespace, E*Trade, State Farm, Allstate, Barclaycard, Microsoft, webuyanycar.com, Just Eat, Franklin Templeton, ICICI and others);
- case studies: Google "Parisian Love", Duolingo, Airbnb and Headspace.

Full analysis: `../sources/genre-datasets/apps_services-dataset-analysis.md`. This is the genre for Turito.

## What wins

- **One idea, stated as the viewer's "because":** "they buy any car" (webuyanycar); "saves me money on fees" (E*Trade); "protects my money and future" (Allstate, Franklin Templeton); "helps my business grow" (Google Chrome); "find exactly what I want, fast" (Just Eat).
- **Dramatise the benefit, because services are invisible:**
  - Barclaycard's waterslide means contactless is easy.
  - In Mastercard's "Elephant", the card is so easy even an elephant can use it. **The joke is the proposition.**
- **Show the product doing its job:**
  - **Google Play:** devices syncing, with copy that simply describes it: "Your music, movies, books and apps, anywhere you go".
  - **Google "Parisian Love":** a whole love story told in search queries, the product *is* the story. It was the most-searched Super Bowl ad, with 7M views in a week.
  - **Google Chrome "Frank's Restaurant":** a human story about its usefulness.
- **Funny and exciting are fuel, not the engine.** Eff-5 ads averaged funny 0.61 and exciting 0.59, but weak ones were often just as funny (Berlitz "We are sinking", Squarespace with Key & Peele, both rated 3). **Squarespace is the cautionary tale:** stylish ads made for art directors, where viewers didn't know what they were buying.
- **Empowerment beats fear:** "inspired" and "confident" worked (Google Chrome); "afraid" (Identity Guard) was weaker.
- **Durable platforms:** State Farm's "Like a good neighbor" (the jingle is the trigger, the agent appearing is the gag, the benefit is the payoff) and Mastercard "Priceless".
- **App-native entertainment:** Duolingo's funny, story-led formats reached 96% completion.

## Rules

1. **Fill in "use it because ___" in one short, tangible clause before writing anything.**
2. **One ad, one idea:** ease *or* security *or* access.
3. **Dramatise the invisible benefit** with a metaphor or a situation.
4. **The punchline is the product benefit.**
5. **Keep the copy crystal clear:** say what you offer, plainly.
6. **Show the real product working:** real UI screens as composited inserts, never AI-drawn UI.
7. **Build a repeatable platform** (a jingle, a character, a format).
8. **Lead with empowerment and outcome** (the student who now understands), not fear.

## Craft

- **The real screen insert:** a clean, full-frame screen with the key moment animating (our pipeline already renders these from real screenshots, with a reveal).
- **The problem, then the turn:** the person's moment of friction, then the product, then the relief or result. Keep the human moment simple and short (AI faces are risky); faceless alternatives are hands, over-the-shoulder shots and screen POV.
- **Text is part of the design here** (the CTA and the offer), but it's typeset in the edit and matches the voice-over word for word.
- **CTA:** specific and single ("Start free", "Book a demo"), heard and seen, with "limited" if there's a real offer.
