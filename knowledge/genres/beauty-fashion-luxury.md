# Genre: Beauty, fashion and luxury

**Evidence:**
- 131 famous-brand ads in the Pitt dataset (Nike, Dove, Victoria's Secret, Old Spice, Axe, Chanel, Gucci, Prada, Levi's, Under Armour, Fabletics, Nair and others);
- case studies: Dove "Real Beauty", Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like", and Chanel No. 5.

Full analysis: `../sources/genre-datasets/beauty_fashion-dataset-analysis.md`.

## What wins

- **Sub-genres run on different engines:**
  - **Men's grooming** (Old Spice, Axe): surreal, high-energy **humour** selling social success. Funny scores run 0.8–1.0 and the sentiment is *amused*. Old Spice body wash sales rose **107%** in the month after launch, and the brief targeted **women**, who make 60% of body wash purchases.
  - **Sportswear** (Nike, Under Armour, Saucony): **excitement** and inspiration, selling performance. "You will elevate your game." The celebrity shows the discipline (Tom Brady in "Rule Yourself"); it isn't just an endorsement.
  - **Self-esteem beauty** (Dove): **emotion** and a cultural truth ("4% of women call themselves beautiful"). Rated 5/5 despite low exciting and funny scores; the tone matched the message.
  - **Luxury** (Chanel, Gucci): **status and sensory cinema**. Chanel's print was the bottle, the name and almost nothing else. Its films feel like prestige short films. The best Gucci ad tied the look to a feeling ("confidence to be successful"). Weak luxury ads are "moving lookbooks" that sell a vibe but no feeling.
- **The takeaway:** don't chase funny or exciting. **Chase alignment**: the tone must serve the proposition.

## Rules

1. **A single, tangible proposition:** Fabletics "your privates won't show", not "great leggings"; Nair "you can wear short shorts".
2. **Turn every feature into what it does for you.**
3. **The celebrity is the vehicle, not the destination:** show what they achieve with the product.
4. **For sportswear, promise performance** (what you can *do*).
5. **For luxury, restraint:** minimal copy, the product and the name, "luxury doesn't over-explain". The film must still attach one feeling.
6. **For grooming, go bold and absurd,** and make the joke deliver the promise.
7. **For self-esteem, lead with a researched truth** and let the product be the act of agreeing with it.

## Craft

- **Sensorial close-ups:** texture swatches (cream, serum drops), fabric in motion, light on glass, skin with real texture (never plastic), and slow, elegant pours.
- **Luxury grammar:** slow, deliberate moves, negative space, controlled contrast, a single hero object, and a muted, rich grade.
- **Sound:** intimate foley (the cap click, the spray, the fabric rustle), with a refined score. For luxury, a solo instrument; for sport, driving rhythm.
- **AI notes:**
  - Logos and bottle text warp in motion, so composite the real bottle or pack.
  - Liquids and sprays need several attempts.
  - Faces are the hardest part, so faceless options lead: the product, hands, silhouettes, fabric and motion.
