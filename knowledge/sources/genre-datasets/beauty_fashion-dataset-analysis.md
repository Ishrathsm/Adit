# beauty_fashion: what separates effective famous-brand ads (Pitt dataset, n=131)

Alright, team. Listen up. We've got a pile of data here on what actually cuts through in beauty and fashion. Let's break it down so our next brief is sharp enough to cut glass. Pay attention, this is how we win.

**A quick note:** This is a small sample (~130 ads, ~5 viewers each), so these are strong signals, not gospel. But the patterns are clear.

### 1. What the most effective ads (eff 5) have in common

The top-tier ads aren't just pretty; they are machines built to deliver a single, powerful message. They do this with three things:

**A. A Razor-Sharp Proposition:** Viewers know *exactly* what the product will do for them. This falls into three categories:
*   **Functional Superiority:** The product just *works better*.
    *   **Fabletics "See Through":** The proposition is crystal clear: "your private parts will not show because of the quality material." It's a direct, functional promise addressing a real customer fear.
    *   **Listerine "BringOutTheBold":** The benefit is tangible: "it will protect your gums against any type of food."
    *   **Citizen "L Sunrise":** A unique, memorable feature: "it needs no batteries, only light."
*   **Emotional Transformation:** Using the product changes how you *feel* about yourself.
    *   **Dove "#MyBeautyMySay":** The promise isn't soap, it's empowerment. Viewers repeated back: "YOU WILL FEEL COMFORTABLE IN YOUR SKIN," and "they encourage self esteem."
    *   **Saucony "FIND YOUR STRONG":** It's not about cushioning; it's about inner strength. Viewers heard: "they make you strong," and "strength is all you have left."
*   **Social Enhancement:** Using the product improves your status or attractiveness to others.
    *   **Axe "Dark Temptation":** The metaphor is the message. You become "as irresistible to women as chocolate."
    *   **Levi's "laundry":** The jeans aren't just a fit, they're a tool: "Levi's will make me get attention from others."

**B. The Right Device for the Job:**
*   **Humour:** Used brilliantly to deliver a promise of social success, especially for men. **Old Spice ("Neil Patrick Harris")** and **Axe ("AXE Commercial")** use comedy to make the claim "this will make you attractive" feel entertaining, not arrogant.
*   **Inspirational Story:** The go-to for building a brand ethos. **Nike's "No Excuses"** (Matt Scott) isn't about shoes; it's about destroying excuses. The product becomes a symbol of that mindset. **Dove's "Real Beauty"** films do the same for self-worth.
*   **Celebrity as Proof:** The best ads don't just show a celebrity; they show them *achieving the promise*. **Under Armour's "Rule Yourself"** shows Tom Brady's insane discipline, making the gear feel like part of his success system. This is far more powerful than just seeing a famous person in a shirt.
*   **Metaphor/Analogy:** Simplifies a complex idea. **Axe's "Dark Temptation"** (chocolate man) is the peak example. It's instantly understood and memorable.

**C. A Tone that Matches the Promise:**
The highest-rated ads align their tone perfectly. Sportswear ads like **Saucony** and **Nike** feel `active` and `inspired`. Men's grooming ads like **Old Spice** and **Axe** are overwhelmingly `amused`. Luxury ads like **Prada** and **Chanel** are `fashionable`. Dove’s are `emotional` or `feminine`. There is no single "best" tone; there is only the *right* tone for the message.

### 2. What the weakest ads (eff ≤ 3) do wrong

The weak ads are a masterclass in wasted budgets. They fail for two main reasons:

*   **Vague, Watery Propositions:** They traffic in empty ad-speak that viewers can't grasp.
    *   **JD Sports "#EpicFinish" (eff 2):** What does "it will help you determine or create your epic finish" even mean? It’s a meaningless, unmemorable platitude. Contrast this with Nike’s “elevate your game,” which is a concrete performance promise.
*   **Lazy Celebrity Endorsement:** They rent a famous face and hope the magic rubs off. It doesn't.
    *   **Reebok "allen iverson A7" (eff 1):** Viewers say, "Allen Iverson represents that brand." So what? It’s a statement of fact, not a reason to buy. There’s no transfer of Iverson’s *skill* or *attitude* to the consumer. Compare this to the eff 5 **Nike "Melo"** ad, where viewers said the shoes "will help you elevate to the next level." One is a poster, the other is a promise.
*   **Atmosphere Over Substance:** They create a cool vibe but forget to sell anything.
    *   **Gucci "Spring Summer 2016" (eff 2):** The ad is fashionable, but the viewer takeaway is generic: "you can party." It doesn't give a specific reason to choose Gucci over any other luxury brand. The eff 5 **Gucci "Men's Tailoring"** ad, by contrast, promised "the confidence to be successful." That’s a real benefit.

### 3. The propositions viewers actually repeat back

Forget your marketing jargon. This is the language that lands in people’s heads. Here are the top 10 clearest "because..." reasons from this dataset:

1.  **Because it will improve my performance** (run faster, play better, elevate my game). - *Nike, Under Armour*
2.  **Because it will make me attractive to women.** - *Axe, Old Spice, Hai Karate*
3.  **Because it promotes self-esteem and individuality.** - *Dove*
4.  **Because it is high-quality material that solves a specific problem** (e.g., see-through leggings, shrink-free jeans). - *Fabletics, Levi's*
5.  **Because successful pros use it to win.** - *Nike, Under Armour*
6.  **Because it will make me look/feel sexy.** - *Victoria's Secret, Armani*
7.  **Because it will make me smell manly/fresh.** - *Old Spice*
8.  **Because it will help me achieve my fitness goals/be more active.** - *Nike, Old Navy*
9.  **Because it has a specific, smart feature** (e.g., solar-powered, long-lasting). - *Citizen, Enjoli*
10. **Because it will give me confidence.** - *Gucci, Sunsilk*

### 4. Do exciting / funny / particular sentiments help here?

Yes, but only when they are weapons chosen for a specific strategy. They are not a strategy in themselves.

*   **Funny:** A score of 0.8-1.0 is a hallmark of the top-performing men's grooming ads (**Old Spice, Axe, Hai Karate**). The sentiment is always `amused`. In this sub-genre, humour is the delivery mechanism for the "social success" promise. It works.
*   **Exciting:** A score of 0.8-1.0 is all over the effective sportswear ads (**Saucony, Under Armour, Nike**). The sentiment is `active` or `inspired`. Excitement signals high performance and energy, perfectly matching the proposition.
*   **Sentiment Alignment is Key:** An ad doesn't need to be funny *or* exciting to be a 5/5. **Dove's "Real Beauty"** was an eff 5 with low exciting (0.4) and funny (0.2) scores, but its `emotional` sentiment was a perfect match for its self-esteem message. **Men's Wearhouse** scored a 5 with a `calm` tone, reinforcing its message of quiet, detail-oriented quality.

**The takeaway:** Don't chase "funny" or "exciting." Chase *alignment*. The tone must serve the proposition.

### 5. Brand-specific patterns worth copying

*   **Nike vs. Dove: Selling the Ethos vs. the Emotion.**
    *   **Nike** is the ultimate coach. Every ad, whether using a megastar like LeBron ("The Beginning") or an unknown hero like Matt Scott ("No Excuses"), is about *unlocking human potential*. The proposition is always a version of "we give you the tools to be better." Their celebrities are living proof of that ethos.
    *   **Dove** is the ultimate confidante. They sell *emotional validation*. Their ads ("#MyBeautyMySay," "Real Beauty") challenge cultural norms and tell the viewer, "you are beautiful as you are." The proposition is self-acceptance. The product is almost incidental; buying it is an act of alignment with that mission.

*   **Old Spice/Axe vs. Luxury Fashion (Gucci/Prada): Selling Social Success vs. Status.**
    *   **Old Spice** and **Axe** sell blue-collar social mobility. The promise is simple and primal: use our spray, get the girl. They use surreal, high-energy humour (**"I'm on a Horse," "Chocolate Man"**) to make this claim entertaining and disarming.
    *   **Gucci** and **Prada** sell elite status. The promise is sophistication and confidence. The best ads, like **Gucci's "Men's Tailoring,"** connect the aesthetic to a feeling of power ("confidence to be successful"). The weaker ones are just moving lookbooks that sell a vibe but no tangible feeling.

### 6. 10 concrete rules for writing an ad in this genre

1.  **Have a Single, Tangible Proposition.** Don't be vague. Be brutally specific about the benefit.
    *   *Example:* **Fabletics "See Through."** The promise isn't "great leggings," it's "your private parts will not show."

2.  **Translate Every Feature into a Benefit.** Nobody buys a feature. They buy what the feature *does for them*.
    *   *Example:* **Nair "1980s."** The feature is hair removal. The benefit is "you will be able to wear short shorts."

3.  **Make the Celebrity a Vehicle, Not the Destination.** The ad isn't about them; it's about what they *achieve* with your product.
    *   *Example:* **Under Armour "Rule Yourself."** The ad isn't "Tom Brady wears our stuff." It's "This is the discipline it takes to be Tom Brady, and our gear is part of that system."

4.  **Promise Performance, Not Just an Endorsement.** For sportswear, it's about what the user can *do*.
    *   *Example:* **Nike "Melo Commercial."** The promise isn't just that Melo wears them, it's that "you will elevate your game with these sneakers."

5.  **When Promising Social Success, Use Humour.** A direct claim of "this makes you attractive" is arrogant. A funny, exaggerated claim is charming.
    *   *Example:* **Axe "New AXE Dark Temptation."** The proposition "it will make you as irresistible to women as chocolate" is delivered via a surreal, hilarious visual.

6.  **Own an Emotion or a Mission.** If you can't be functionally superior, be emotionally essential.
    *   *Example:* **Dove "Real Beauty."** The mission is promoting self-esteem. The soap is the proof you believe in it.

7.  **If You Have a Demo, Make It Unforgettable.** Show the product working in a simple, dramatic way.
    *   *Example:* **Listerine "Bold Team."** They test their "PinkStrong Gums" against extreme foods. It’s a literal demonstration of the promise.

8.  **The Tagline IS the Strategy.** It should be the shortest possible summary of your proposition.
    *   *Example:* **Nike "No Excuses."** The tagline is literally the takeaway viewers repeated: "Because there is no excuse not to."

9.  **Vague Aspiration is a Death Trap.** Avoid empty phrases like "find your style" or "live your moment." Give them a concrete outcome.
    *   *Bad Example:* **JD Sports "#EpicFinish."** "Help you create your epic finish."
    *   *Good Example:* **Gucci "Men's Tailoring."** "Give you the confidence to be successful."

10. **Write the Tone into the Script.** The feeling of the ad is as important as the words. A funny VO for a sportswear ad is as wrong as a serious VO for an Old Spice ad.
    *   *Example:* The `amused` tone of **Old Spice "Neil Patrick Harris"** is essential to its success. You can't separate the copy from the performance.
