### 2. MOTION RULES

1.  **Coupled Motion is Key:** When the bike and camera both move (the majority of riding shots), they are perfectly coupled. The camera tracks at the bike's exact speed, keeping the bike locked in the same position within the frame (e.g., center, left third). The visual dynamism comes from the background moving, not the subject.
2.  **Simple Paths:** All motion paths, for both camera and bike, are either straight lines or single, smooth, wide arcs. There are no complex S-curves or jerky movements.
3.  **Static Location:** The location never changes within a single shot. A shot is either in a tunnel, on a road, or in a plaza, never transitioning between them.
4.  **Distinct Motion States:** Shots are either `STATIC` (camera and bike locked), `CAMERA-ONLY` (slow tracks/pans on static bike details), or `COUPLED-MOTION` (riding shots). There are very few shots where only the bike moves through a locked-off frame.

### 3. EDIT

*   **Pacing:** Fast. Most shots are 1-2 seconds long. Detail shots are the shortest (1s), while establishing or riding shots can extend to 2-3s.
*   **Cut Timing:** Cuts are timed aggressively to the beat of the rock music. Action-to-action cuts are used to create a sense of continuous forward momentum, even when locations change. A look at the bike cuts to a detail of what is being looked at.
*   **Shot-Size Progression:** The edit follows a classic funnel: `Intimate Lifestyle Details (CU) -> Human Context (MS) -> Product Reveal (WS) -> Product Details (ECU) -> Product in Action (Dynamic MS/WS) -> Product Family Lineup (WS)`.
*   **Reveal & Logo:** The motorcycle is revealed slowly—first as a background object, then in detail, then as a whole static object, and only then does it move. The Triumph brand logo is on screen in the upper-right corner for the entire duration, reinforcing the brand before the final logo-only end card.

### 4. SOUND

*   **Music:** Mid-tempo, blues-based garage rock. It starts with a simple, confident guitar riff and builds energy by adding drums and bass as the rider prepares to leave. The energy peaks during the riding sequence and resolves on a final power chord.
*   **Sound Effects:** Effects are punchy, stylized, and perfectly synced. The most important SFX is the **engine start**, which acts as a major transition from the static first act to the dynamic second act. Engine sounds during riding are present but mixed cleanly *under* the dominant music track.
*   **Voice-Over:** A deep, male, authoritative voice. The VO is mixed to sit clearly on top of the music. The script sells heritage, quality, and feeling ("the difference")—not technical specifications.
*   **Mix:** Music is the primary bed. VO is the top layer. SFX are accents that punch through the mix at key moments (key-in, engine start, tire squeal).

### 5. The 10 Most Important, Copyable Rules for an AI Pipeline

1.  **Clip Duration:** Generate clips that are 1.5 to 3.0 seconds long.
2.  **The "Tracking Rule":** For all moving shots, the camera must `track alongside at constant speed`, keeping the motorcycle in a fixed position (center or rule-of-thirds) within the frame. The background provides the motion.
3.  **The "Detail Rule":** Generate `slow track in` or `slow pan` clips on static Extreme Close-Ups (ECU) of bike details: headlight, engine fins, tank logo, seat stitching.
4.  **The "Start-Up Moment":** Generate a static Medium Shot (MS) of the rider on the bike, head-on. This clip is for the powerful "engine start" sound effect.
5.  **Shallow Depth of Field:** All clips must be rendered with a very shallow DoF. The subject (bike/rider) is sharp; the background is heavily blurred (bokeh).
6.  **Golden Hour Lighting:** All exterior shots must use a warm, low-angle light source to simulate sunrise or sunset, creating long shadows and lens flare.
7.  **Motion Path Simplicity:** All movement (camera or bike) must follow either a straight line or a single, gentle arc.
8.  **Consistent Location:** A single clip must take place in one discrete location (e.g., "tunnel," "tree-lined road," "urban plaza"). Do not blend locations within a clip.
9.  **Human Prep Shots:** Include static or `slow push-in` MS clips of a rider preparing: putting on a helmet, zipping a jacket, pulling on gloves.
10. **The Final Lineup:** The final clip must be a static Wide Shot (WS) showing a family of 3-5 motorcycles parked side-by-side in front of a clean, premium background (e.g., a modern building or hotel entrance).
