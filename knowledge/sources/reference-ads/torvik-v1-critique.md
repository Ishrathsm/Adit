### Final Verdict & Fixes

**Overall Realism Score: 3/10**

It starts strong with static shots but falls apart completely the moment things start moving. The AI understands "cool picture of a bike" but not "how to film a moving object." It breaks the most fundamental rules of vehicle cinematography and product advertising.

**Top 5 Fixes to Make This Not Look Like Garbage:**

1.  **LOCK THE DESIGN:** This is non-negotiable. One bike. One engine color. One exhaust. One fork color. You're selling a specific product, not the *idea* of a motorcycle. The AI must be trained on a single, consistent 3D model or set of reference photos.
2.  **HARD CUTS ONLY:** Kill all morphs and dissolves between action shots. They scream "fake." A cut from the garage to the street is powerful. A dissolve is weak. Cut on the beat. Snap, snap, snap.
3.  **OBEY THE LAWS OF PHYSICS (AND CAMERAS):** Every moving shot must adhere to Rule #1. Either the camera is on a dolly/crane/car mount tracking perfectly *with* the bike (bike is stable in frame), or the camera is locked down and the bike passes *through* the frame. The "both moving independently" floaty nonsense needs to be eliminated entirely.
4.  **IMPROVE PACING:** The action shots are too long. A good car/bike ad uses quick, 1-2 second cuts that give a feeling of speed and energy. The longer cuts here just give the viewer more time to see the flaws and the fake-looking CGI backgrounds.
5.  **CHOOSE A LOCATION, STICK WITH IT:** The jump from a moody blue city to a golden-hour forest is jarring. While real ads do this, the AI's execution makes it feel like it's just grabbing random assets. A real shoot would unify these with a consistent color grade and visual style. The AI needs to learn visual cohesion across an entire sequence, not just within a single shot.
