### 2. MOTION RULES

1.  **Coupling is Key:** The most realistic shots have the camera and bike moving together. The camera either **tracks alongside the bike at the exact same speed** (bike stays fixed in frame, background blurs) or **tracks backward as the bike approaches** (creating a dynamic, engaging shot). Static camera shots are used for establishing turns or jumps where the bike moves *through* the frame.
2.  **Straight Line Principle:** Within any single clip, the bike travels in a straight line or a single, clean arc. The complexity of the "journey" is created by **cutting together different straight-line shots**, not by complex maneuvers within one shot.
3.  **No In-Shot Location Change:** A shot is *on the street*, *on the roof*, or *in an alley*. The location never changes within the shot. The cut is what transports the viewer and the bike. This is a critical rule for clip-based generation.

### 3. EDIT

*   **Pacing Curve:** Starts slow (1 shot in 5s), accelerates into a frantic pace (1-2s per shot), then resolves with longer, smoother shots (3-4s).
*   **Cut Triggers:** Cuts are timed to the percussive hits of the electronic music beat. Action cuts (like landing a jump) are emphasized with a synced sound effect that hits precisely on the frame of the cut. The "Rerouting..." voiceover often acts as a pre-lap, starting just before a cut to a new, unexpected location.
*   **Shot Progression:** The sequence is: **Problem** (Medium shots in traffic) -> **Decision** (Close-ups on phone/face) -> **Action/Solution** (Rapid-fire mix of wide, tracking, and POV shots) -> **Triumph/Reveal** (Smooth, wide drone shots of the bike in a clean environment).
*   **Product Reveal:** The bike's name and features are held until the very end, after the action peak. The final shot is a "hero" shot, showcasing the bike's aesthetics cleanly.

### 4. SOUND

*   **Music:** Driving, percussive electronic track. It's more about rhythm and energy than melody. The energy builds to a crescendo during the bridge sequence and then fades out with the logo.
*   **Effects Design:** Hyper-realistic and punchy. Engine revs, tire screeches, and landing "thuds" are exaggerated and mixed high in the audio. They serve as punctuation for the edit.
*   **Effect Sync:** Sound effects are precisely synced to on-screen action and cuts. A "whoosh" covers a pan, a "thud" hits on a landing, a "rev" hits on an acceleration.
*   **Voice:** A calm, female, robotic AI voice. Its monotony contrasts sharply with the visual chaos, creating a "cool under pressure" technological feel.
*   **Mix:** The music provides a constant bed of energy, but the SFX and VO punch through it at key moments. Ambient street noise is only present in the opening shot.

### 5. The 10 Most Important, Copyable AI Rules

1.  **Generate 1-2s clips for action.** All action sequences are built from ultra-short clips cut together on a musical beat. Longer 4-5s clips are only for establishing and closing shots.
2.  **Use two primary motion templates:**
    *   **Template A (Tracking):** Camera moves at the bike's exact speed (side or front 3/4 angle), keeping the bike locked in-frame while the background streaks by.
    *   **Template B (Pass-By):** Camera is locked or does a slow pan as the bike enters and exits the frame. Use this for turns and jumps.
3.  **Motion must be a single vector.** Within one clip, the bike moves in one direction (e.g., straight forward, a single left turn). Do not generate S-curves or direction changes within a single clip.
4.  **Sync cuts to a percussive SFX.** Every cut in the action sequence must land on a sound: an engine rev, a tire screech, a landing thud, or the "Rerouting" voice.
5.  **Intersperse POV shots.** Every 3-4 action shots, insert a 2-second POV clip from the rider's perspective, showing the handlebars and instrument cluster/phone.
6.  **Alternate angles constantly.** Never use the same angle twice in a row. Sequence: low-angle -> high-angle -> rear 3/4 -> POV -> side-on.
7.  **Isolate stunts.** A wheelie is one short clip (low-angle, front). A jump is one short clip (side-on). A landing is one short clip (low-angle, front).
8.  **The environment is static within a clip.** A clip is either "rooftop" or "alley." The cut creates the teleportation effect.
9.  **Use a robotic female VO as a transition device.** Place the "Rerouting..." soundbite across the cut between two different locations to justify the sudden change.
10. **End with a "Hero" drone shot.** The final clip must be a 4-5s smooth drone shot, tracking the bike from a wide angle in a scenic, open location (e.g., a bridge). The music should resolve here.
